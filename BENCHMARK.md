Additional September 27 smoke runs found and fixed three image-path problems: a `Blob` was passed to `ImageDecoder` for static WebP, authenticated same-origin image refetches omitted page cookies, and excess queued images could remain black after a capacity response. The updated browser checks revealed a static WebP, a cookie-protected JPEG, and all 12 extra images queued behind the fixture media. An immediate nested-scroll geometry check measured zero difference between the media and anchored overlay bounds. These are functional checks, not censorship-coverage measurements.

A later 24-image grid check verified viewport-only scheduling: the first visible row completed in top-to-bottom, left-to-right order (`grid-0`, `grid-1`, `grid-2`), zero images below the viewport had been analyzed at that point, and `grid-18` was processed after scrolling into view. Removing `grid-0` also removed its overlay. This is a synthetic layout check, not a live Google Images compatibility claim. That build kept one image analysis in flight per frame; the September 29 optimization allows two image pipelines while preserving viewport priority.

Subsequent profiling isolated the older slow path: MediaPipe CPU semantic segmentation took roughly 500 ms per frame, while warm GPU segmentation on this setup took roughly 90–130 ms. The first GPU segmentation still spent about 6.7 seconds compiling; the initial bus image took about 8 seconds end to end. The packaged ONNX Runtime Web WASM build now uses its external WASM files, reducing `person-runtime.js` from about 58 MB to 67 KB. These timings describe this local headless setup, not a general hardware guarantee.

The mixed image/video run used 320-pixel YOLO input for images and 256 pixels for video, retaining both model sessions. Over ten seconds, 35 video captures completed; p95 capture-to-result age was 300 ms, and the video was masked in 92/100 sampled moments. The visible first row of a 24-image grid finished in 409 ms after warmup; zero below-viewport images had been analyzed, and an image farther down was processed after scrolling. The video still entered `stale` briefly, so the result does not meet the design's unexpected-blackout-duty target. The bus video used conservative `uncertain-person` region protection; selected-region accuracy at 256 pixels is not validated.

These video measurements come from a headless smoke run, not the required 300-analysis, three-repetition, 15-minute benchmark. The longer fixture advanced about ten media seconds during ten wall-clock seconds and produced 35 captures, but this still cannot establish regular Chrome playback performance. The observed p95 just reaches the design's 300 ms age target; it does not establish a repeatable release pass. Accuracy, label transfer, blackout duty, memory, dropped frames, and real-site compatibility gates remain open.

## September 28 motion and exemption changes

The worker now keeps a usable explicit label through a brief lost-track interval and restores it only after a strong, unambiguous match. Conservative masks are tied to selected people; disabled face skin and clothing no longer enter the unassigned-person mask. Small disconnected mask artifacts are removed before a one-pixel edge expansion. On the bus image smoke fixture, the rendered mask fell from 14,672 to 6,859 display pixels; assigning one male label reduced it to 3,300. Other unlabelled people remain censored. This is a behavioral check, not an annotated coverage score.

Motion correction runs in a packaged worker inside an extension-origin iframe. A transferable, resized `ImageBitmap` is created asynchronously; page code predicts layer positions at every presented video frame. The worker processed 131 motion frames while 298 video frames were presented in a ten-second headless sample, with prediction between corrections. Page-thread motion work was 0.9 ms p95; worker motion processing was 6.5 ms p95. The video had a visible mask in 95/100 sampled moments, 13 samples used a propagated prior mask, and p95 model result age was 278 ms. The remaining black samples and motion accuracy require further inspection; this is not a claim of full-frame-rate pixel analysis or zero blackout.

A second combined smoke run also checked nested scrolling, an explicit male image label, and viewport image ordering. It showed masks in 94/100 video samples, with the six black samples at indices 0–5 before the first usable video result; no black sample occurred later in that run. The motion worker processed 141 of 298 presented frames; model result age was 353 ms p95, page-thread motion work 0.9 ms p95, and worker motion processing 7.4 ms p95. A separate smoke check applied a male label to a playing-video track. These synthetic checks do not prove live-site mask alignment or eliminate blackouts during slower inference, scene changes, or failures.

A pause-event smoke check kept the previous video mask visible immediately after pausing, while fresh analysis of the paused frame was requested. Explicit male labelling also passed in both the still-image and playing-video smoke paths after the label control began reporting failed assignments instead of silently closing.

## September 28 face-detector replacement

The previous full-range BlazeFace path resized a four-tile person-head grid into one MediaPipe detector input, reducing face detail. YuNet 2026may now runs once on a 320×320 view of the full 640×640 face capture. Its boxes and nose landmark use normalized coordinates; FastFace still receives crops from the 640-pixel capture. OpenCV Zoo reports YuNet WIDER Face validation AP of 0.8844/0.8656/0.7503 (easy/medium/hard). InsightFace reports 0.9380/0.9202/0.7713 for SCRFD 2.5G with keypoints, although its published setup and licensing differ. These are upstream results, not this extension's accuracy.

On this machine, native ONNX Runtime warm inference medians were roughly 2.6 ms at 320 and 24 ms at 640 for YuNet, versus 21.7 ms and 69.8 ms for the tested SCRFD 2.5G artifact. On the four included portrait/group fixtures, both candidates found the same clear faces at both sizes. The packaged Chromium YuNet probe at 320 found the Lena face, two small bus faces, and both side-facing portraits; warm per-image detection took roughly 28–50 ms in that short run, with 142 ms for its first measured image. The full extension found and associated ten faces in the repeated-person group fixture and correctly associated the side-facing woman after allowing an unowned profile nose pixel. These small tests justify YuNet's speed/coverage choice here, but do not prove a large real-world recall gain over BlazeFace. A labelled, varied face-detection set and live-site timing remain open gates.

The generic video smoke also reached the new detector and classified two bus faces without a script error. Its sampled video result was `stale` at 831 ms total, with 671 ms spent in person segmentation; this run does not pass the 500 ms video freshness limit. YuNet improved face detection cost, but it cannot by itself resolve that separate YOLO/video performance limit.

## September 29 image-throughput work

The extension keeps the same packaged YOLO, MediaPipe semantic, YuNet, and FastFace models, input resolutions, and detection thresholds. The content script now prepares the engine while it acquires images and allows two image pipelines at once. The capture path and face/semantic preprocessing reuse canvas buffers; the image result carries one combined mask. YOLO prototype-mask decoding reuses each sampled prototype pixel within a detection, reducing the bus probe's mask decode from about 78 ms to about 15 ms before the later isolated test. A bounded in-memory cache reuses model outputs only for byte-identical still frames under the same inference settings. It never persists frames and creates fresh per-image tracks.

The offscreen scheduler now favors visible images while allowing video after two consecutive images. It clears completed-job timers, transfers newly allocated mask buffers, and only invokes semantic CPU fallback for semantic failures. GPU semantic execution and the ONNX WASM path remain the default; an experimental ONNX WebGPU attempt timed out in this headless environment and was not made the default. Running semantic segmentation in another nested worker gave 1.63 images/s versus 1.87 images/s in the corresponding short local-worker trial, so that path is disabled by default.

On this i5-8250U, the `scripts/perf.mjs` headless Chromium photo-grid benchmark generated varied 480-pixel JPEG crops of the four included fixtures, displayed them at 200 pixels, and ran with the default 2 ONNX threads. Each of three rounds analyzed 100 images, with **zero cache hits**. The rounds took 40.3, 31.8, and 30.7 seconds: **2.48, 3.15, and 3.26 images/s**. Warm-round per-image reveal latency was 589–595 ms at p50 and 742–828 ms at p95. Mean warm person-model run time was 226–232 ms/image, semantic segmentation 103–105 ms, and face plus gender about 55–60 ms; some stages overlap, so these components should not be summed to estimate total latency. The first round includes cold model/GPU startup and reached a 987 ms p95, but its slowest image is not captured by that percentile.

The new **ONNX CPU threads** popup option supports Auto, 1, 2, 4, 6, 8, 12, and 16. A popup smoke test changed to 8 and confirmed a new bus analysis reported 8 threads. In separate short warm 12-image runs, 2 threads completed at 2.70 images/s while 8 threads completed at 2.30 images/s on this four-core/eight-thread laptop. Larger counts are therefore opt-in; Auto resolves to 2 here. These are short synthetic comparisons, not a ranking for other CPUs.

The 300-image run, ten-person browser smoke, popup thread-change smoke, and viewport grid smoke pass without changing model behavior. A 15-minute timed grid run then completed **30 batches of 100 images (3,000 total)** with zero cache hits and no reported browser page errors. The batches totaled 893 seconds of processing, or **3.36 images/s** overall. After the cold first batch at 2.16 images/s, warm batches reached 3.84 images/s. Batches 25–29 temporarily slowed to 2.74–3.08 images/s while mean YOLO run time rose from roughly 200 ms to 238–266 ms; the final batch recovered to 3.58 images/s. CPU temperature and competing system load were not captured, so the slowdown's cause is unknown.

A separate ten-second long-video smoke completed with no errors but showed a visible mask in 91/100 samples and p95 result age of 543 ms, above the 500 ms freshness limit; most black samples were at startup and one occurred later. Packaged Chrome did not load the extension service worker in this headless run, so the repeatable grid figures use Playwright Chromium. Regular Chrome, image-search websites, visual-mask equivalence on annotated media, and memory pressure have not been validated here. The remaining throughput limit is primarily warm YOLO inference (about 200–270 ms/image in the sustained run), with semantic and face/gender work also consuming CPU/GPU time. More ONNX threads alone did not remove that limit.

## September 29 model timing and resolution controls

Before adding resolution controls, a fresh 100-image warm benchmark at the existing default resolutions (YOLO image 320, YuNet 320, FastFace 128, MediaPipe semantic 256; 640-pixel face source) measured mean inference-call times of **262 ms YOLO**, **108 ms MediaPipe semantic**, **23 ms YuNet**, and **28 ms FastFace** per processed image. It used two ONNX threads in headless Chromium on the same i5-8250U and had zero cache hits. Its whole warm batch took 36.1 seconds (2.77 images/s). The first, cold 100-image batch measured 368, 204, 24, and 29 ms respectively, but cold compilation and initialization are not included in those per-call means. The 15-minute run above shows normal variation in YOLO timing, so these are measurements of this run, not constants for every image or computer. YuNet and FastFace can be skipped when no face/person needs classification; FastFace batches eligible faces, so its per-image time depends on face count. YOLO and semantic stages can overlap.

Version 0.4.0 adds independent YOLO image/video resolution controls using only the three packaged static exports (256, 320, 416). YuNet accepts 256, 320, or 416 in the popup; face source capture accepts 320, 416, 512, or 640 before YuNet resize and FastFace crop extraction. MediaPipe semantic and FastFace model tensors remain fixed at 256 and 128 respectively. Default values preserve the previous effective resolutions. A browser smoke checked a reanalyzed image with YOLO 416, YuNet 256, and 320-pixel face source, then YuNet 416. These controls trade speed against detail; accuracy at every new setting has not been established on annotated media.
