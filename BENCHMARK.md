## September 28 face-detector replacement

The previous full-range BlazeFace path resized a four-tile person-head grid into one MediaPipe detector input, reducing face detail. YuNet 2026may now runs once on a 320×320 view of the full 640×640 face capture. Its boxes and nose landmark use normalized coordinates; FastFace still receives crops from the 640-pixel capture. OpenCV Zoo reports YuNet WIDER Face validation AP of 0.8844/0.8656/0.7503 (easy/medium/hard). InsightFace reports 0.9380/0.9202/0.7713 for SCRFD 2.5G with keypoints, although its published setup and licensing differ. These are upstream results, not this extension's accuracy.

On this machine, native ONNX Runtime warm inference medians were roughly 2.6 ms at 320 and 24 ms at 640 for YuNet, versus 21.7 ms and 69.8 ms for the tested SCRFD 2.5G artifact. On the four included portrait/group fixtures, both candidates found the same clear faces at both sizes. The packaged Chromium YuNet probe at 320 found the Lena face, two small bus faces, and both side-facing portraits; warm per-image detection took roughly 28–50 ms in that short run, with 142 ms for its first measured image. The full extension found and associated ten faces in the repeated-person group fixture and correctly associated the side-facing woman after allowing an unowned profile nose pixel. These small tests justify YuNet's speed/coverage choice here, but do not prove a large real-world recall gain over BlazeFace. A labelled, varied face-detection set and live-site timing remain open gates.

The generic video smoke also reached the new detector and classified two bus faces without a script error. Its sampled video result was `stale` at 831 ms total, with 671 ms spent in person segmentation; this run does not pass the 500 ms video freshness limit. YuNet improved face detection cost, but it cannot by itself resolve that separate YOLO/video performance limit.

## September 29 image-throughput work

The extension keeps the same packaged YOLO, MediaPipe semantic, YuNet, and FastFace models, input resolutions, and detection thresholds. The content script now prepares the engine while it acquires images and allows two image pipelines at once. The capture path and face/semantic preprocessing reuse canvas buffers; the image result carries one combined mask. YOLO prototype-mask decoding reuses each sampled prototype pixel within a detection, reducing the bus probe's mask decode from about 78 ms to about 15 ms before the later isolated test. A bounded in-memory cache reuses model outputs only for byte-identical still frames under the same inference settings. It never persists frames and creates fresh per-image tracks.

The offscreen scheduler now favors visible images while allowing video after two consecutive images. It clears completed-job timers, transfers newly allocated mask buffers, and only invokes semantic CPU fallback for semantic failures. GPU semantic execution and the ONNX WASM path remain the default; an experimental ONNX WebGPU attempt timed out in this headless environment and was not made the default. Running semantic segmentation in another nested worker gave 1.63 images/s versus 1.87 images/s in the corresponding short local-worker trial, so that path is disabled by default.

On this i5-8250U, the `scripts/perf.mjs` headless Chromium photo-grid benchmark generated varied 480-pixel JPEG crops of the four included fixtures, displayed them at 200 pixels, and ran with the default 2 ONNX threads. Each of three rounds analyzed 100 images, with **zero cache hits**. The rounds took 40.3, 31.8, and 30.7 seconds: **2.48, 3.15, and 3.26 images/s**. Warm-round per-image reveal latency was 589–595 ms at p50 and 742–828 ms at p95. Mean warm person-model run time was 226–232 ms/image, semantic segmentation 103–105 ms, and face plus gender about 55–60 ms; some stages overlap, so these components should not be summed to estimate total latency. The first round includes cold model/GPU startup and reached a 987 ms p95, but its slowest image is not captured by that percentile.

The new **ONNX CPU threads** popup option supports Auto, 1, 2, 4, 6, 8, 12, and 16. A popup smoke test changed to 8 and confirmed a new bus analysis reported 8 threads. In separate short warm 12-image runs, 2 threads completed at 2.70 images/s while 8 threads completed at 2.30 images/s on this four-core/eight-thread laptop. Larger counts are therefore opt-in; Auto resolves to 2 here. These are short synthetic comparisons, not a ranking for other CPUs.

The 300-image run, ten-person browser smoke, popup thread-change smoke, and viewport grid smoke pass without changing model behavior. A 15-minute timed grid run then completed **30 batches of 100 images (3,000 total)** with zero cache hits and no reported browser page errors. The batches totaled 893 seconds of processing, or **3.36 images/s** overall. After the cold first batch at 2.16 images/s, warm batches reached 3.84 images/s. Batches 25–29 temporarily slowed to 2.74–3.08 images/s while mean YOLO run time rose from roughly 200 ms to 238–266 ms; the final batch recovered to 3.58 images/s. CPU temperature and competing system load were not captured, so the slowdown's cause is unknown.

A separate ten-second long-video smoke completed with no errors but showed a visible mask in 91/100 samples and p95 result age of 543 ms, above the 500 ms freshness limit; most black samples were at startup and one occurred later. Packaged Chrome did not load the extension service worker in this headless run, so the repeatable grid figures use Playwright Chromium. Regular Chrome, image-search websites, visual-mask equivalence on annotated media, and memory pressure have not been validated here. The remaining throughput limit is primarily warm YOLO inference (about 200–270 ms/image in the sustained run), with semantic and face/gender work also consuming CPU/GPU time. More ONNX threads alone did not remove that limit.

## September 30 YOLO GPU graph capture

The forced WASM backend and unsupported TopK operators in the original GPU graph limited YOLO speed. In addition, starting synchronous local MediaPipe segmentation while awaiting ONNX blocked its continuation/dispatch and counted semantic work inside the reported YOLO wall-clock interval. The new default uses a derived FP32 graph without TopK, a persistent GPU input buffer, GPU capture/replay, and CPU selection equivalent to the original two TopK(300) operations. Local semantic inference now follows YOLO; nested semantic concurrency was slower in this experiment and remains disabled. YuNet and FastFace stay on WASM. GPU output readback is included in `personRun`, and CPU selection is included in `personDecode`. Initialization failures fall back to WASM; worker errors/timeouts retry on WASM. Cold compilation has a 45-second watchdog per new input resolution, while warmed analyses retain eight seconds. Warmup requests no longer create overlapping model sessions.

On this machine's Intel Gen-9 GPU in headless Chromium, using the unchanged 320-pixel model:

The cold batch took 24.207 seconds and had zero cache hits. ## September 30 video coverage follow-up

Moving limbs exposed a weakness in the previous video path: a 256-pixel semantic skin mask was translated as one rigid layer between model samples. Changing an arm's shape cannot be represented by that translation. The video renderer now shields the selected person's entire YOLO-owned silhouette when any region category is enabled and expands its edge by four analysis pixels. This intentionally obscures clothing on selected people during video. Still-image region masks and unselected tracks retain their earlier behavior. Dilation is now a separable two-pass operation so the larger video margin does not require a quadratic-radius neighborhood loop.

Face capture now waits one second instead of 400 ms after all visible tracks have usable labels. Unknown and newly unreliable tracks still request faces at 400 ms. In a ten-second headless long-video run after these changes, the extension processed 25 video analyses while 299 frames were presented. The last sample's YOLO, semantic, face, and gender stages took about 54, 94, 42, and 73 ms respectively. The p95 model-result age was 495 ms, and 88/100 samples had a visible mask; the 12 black samples were at startup. The run had no browser errors. A separate packaged-browser probe with all people selected found 19,817 nontransparent overlay pixels out of 230,400 on a playing frame, confirming that the wider person shield is rendered. All 32 unit tests, typecheck, and build passed.

## Slow-pan motion correction (2026-09-30)

The display-frame motion tracker previously matched only adjacent 96-pixel frames and rounded each displacement to an integer. Camera pans below one analysis pixel per displayed frame repeatedly yielded zero, so the old mask could remain stationary until the next YOLO result. It now matches against an older reference frame until displacement accumulates to two pixels, then moves the reference forward. A synthetic regression pans a textured person region by 0.2 pixel per displayed frame for 40 frames and verifies that the anchored mask follows at least six pixels while remaining reliable. The match ambiguity threshold was relaxed slightly to retain consensus after subpixel interpolation.

A standalone packaged ten-second long-video smoke after this fix completed 35 analyses over 298 displayed frames, with no browser errors. The mask was visible in 96/100 samples, with the other four at startup; p95 result age was 310 ms. Motion-worker p95 was 11.1 ms per processed frame. This fixture is not a calibrated real-camera-pan accuracy test, and motion that changes shape or outruns YOLO still can escape the shield.

## Full-face video shield and faster video path (2026-09-30)

Per the revised requirement, selected video people are now covered across their entire YOLO silhouette, including the face. YuNet face detection and FastFace appearance classification remain active for automatic male/female filtering and exemptions; they no longer cut a hole in the video mask. Video skips the still-image semantic ownership compositor, prunes no small silhouette islands, and renders individual moving mask layers without also building an unused combined RGBA mask. The edge blur uses two one-dimensional passes. Automatic classification retries for unclassified video tracks no more often than every 400 ms, while stable labels retain the one-second interval. Disabling automatic appearance filtering and face diagnostics also skips video face capture and face inference.

The default Balanced preset now requests up to six video analyses per second instead of four, with only one analysis in flight per video. Performance and Quality presets use their existing four and eight analyses-per-second ceilings. The offscreen scheduler prioritizes queued video, reserving an image turn after three consecutive video analyses; a full queue evicts an older image job rather than a video job. This improves video responsiveness on pages with many images but can increase image wait time while video is active.

## Adjustable image and video expansion (2026-09-30)

The popup now exposes independent 0–24 analysis-pixel expansion sliders. Existing installations retain the old fixed defaults of 1 pixel for still-image semantic masks and 4 pixels for video person silhouettes. Image expansion applies to both selected semantic regions owned by a person and unassigned selected semantic regions. Video expansion applies to each selected YOLO person mask before its edge is softened; a zero setting does not paint outside the original silhouette. Slider changes rerender the current analysis rather than rerunning the models. The setting is in model-grid pixels, not CSS or device pixels, so on-screen coverage scales with the chosen input resolution and rendered media size.

A packaged browser check changed the sliders through the popup and confirmed that both values persisted. On the included bus image, raising image expansion from 1 to 8 pixels increased nontransparent overlay pixels from 2,860 to 12,856. On a paused bus video frame, raising video expansion from 4 to 12 pixels increased them from 21,202 to 33,082. This verifies the controls and redraw path, not optimal margins for arbitrary media.

## Whole-body images and masked blur effects (2026-09-30)

Images now offer a second coverage mode that uses the selected YOLO person silhouettes instead of MediaPipe skin and hair. A confidently associated YuNet face opens an oval with a feathered boundary; unassociated or ambiguous faces remain covered. This mode skips semantic inference. Video remains a full-person shield including faces. Both whole-body paths can show black, normal canvas blur, or a pixelated checkerboard mosaic, with independent 8–64 px intensity and grayscale controls. Skin-and-hair images retain only black masking. If drawing source media into an effect canvas fails, the already-drawn black mask remains in place. The checkerboard downsample canvas is reused across equal-sized frames.

A packaged browser smoke with automatic gender disabled and both people selected reported two uniquely associated faces on the bus image. The image whole-body result had `semanticBackend: skipped`; grayscale normal blur produced 36,684 non-black, grayscale-covered pixels. Grayscale checkerboard on the video produced 16,222 non-black grayscale-covered pixels of 20,570 covered pixels in one sampled frame. These checks confirm effect rendering and a face-association input, not that all faces are always revealed or that a given blur intensity makes a person unrecognizable.

This improves protection on ordinary motion but does not guarantee zero exposure on every frame. A fast limb or a newly entering person can move beyond a propagated silhouette before YOLO observes it. Smooth mode deliberately keeps the last mask on screen, so it cannot provide a strict no-leak guarantee. A strict guarantee requires withholding or fully covering frames whenever no current trusted mask exists, which necessarily interrupts normal playback. Cold startup still fully covers video until the first analysis; the 12 black startup samples show that the user's requested combination of uninterrupted visible video and guaranteed zero exposure has not been achieved.

A manual video-label smoke confirms that an assigned male track remains exempt after the silhouette change: masked overlay pixels fell from 19,817 to 13,753 immediately after assignment and remained at 13,729 half a second later. The short three-second fixture loops and resets its tracks, so the probe now checks retention before that reset.

## Optional fast person boxes and tracking (2026-09-30)

Google's [MediaPipe Object Detector guide](https://developers.google.com/edge/mediapipe/solutions/vision/object_detector) lists SSD MobileNetV2 as its faster, smaller choice than EfficientDet-Lite0, but with lower accuracy. Before changing the extension, `experiments/ssd-prototype/benchmark.mjs` ran that official SSD MobileNetV2 float32 model on this machine's headless Chromium: GPU median about 44–47 ms and CPU median about 102–109 ms over warmed 256-pixel runs. It located three clear people on the bus fixture, plus lower-confidence partial detections. This was not a sufficient speed gain over the existing 256-pixel YOLO segmentation path (roughly 54 ms in its isolated WebGPU probe).

The separate YOLO26n detection-only model from the [official Ultralytics release](https://github.com/ultralytics/assets/releases/tag/v8.4.0), exported at 256 pixels, produced four clear bus-fixture person boxes with a 30 ms median and 34.5 ms p95 over 40 warmed WebGPU runs in the isolated probe. It is the implemented optional **Fast person boxes** video backend. Its GPU graph exposes the pre-TopK head; the extension filters and suppresses person boxes locally. A packaged browser GPU/WASM comparison on bus, blank, and bus frames found matching strong person counts (4, 0, 4) and boxes within 0.003 normalized coordinate, confirming that captured GPU input changes between frames. WASM remains an available fallback, though it is slower on this hardware.

On the packaged ten-second moving-video fixture with automatic appearance filtering enabled, fast mode completed 68 analyses over 297 displayed frames, compared with 51 analyses for the default silhouette mode in a separate run. The fast run showed a mask in 96/100 sampled moments, compared with 94/100 for the silhouette run; the other sampled moments were black at cold startup. Its p95 result age was 223 ms versus 169 ms for the silhouette run: increasing analysis cadence made the result-age distribution worse on this older Intel GPU even as it delivered more updates. Full-path per-frame inference under load varied around 40–70 ms, above the 30 ms isolated median. This is a meaningful update-rate gain, not a sustained 30 fps extension pipeline or a promise of zero uncovered motion.

Fast mode uses rectangular person masks with a two-pixel safety margin before the user's video expansion setting. Gender estimation remains enabled, with unique upper-body face/box association. The tracker now considers all viable track/detection pairs, uses a lightweight torso-color histogram to resolve crossings, and accumulates opposing gender estimates over time before changing a committed automatic label. It preserves the label across an unambiguous short detection miss and drops it when a crossing cannot establish identity. Ambiguous or unclassified people stay censored under the default unknown-person policy. Unit tests cover crossing, memory, contrary votes, detector decoding, and face/box ambiguity; packaged browser smoke checked both backends and the popup toggle.

## Standing-to-prone tracking correction (2026-09-30)

A standing person's tall box and push-up pose's wide box can overlap by less than the tracker's 0.3 IoU threshold. The previous association then created a new ID and lost the automatic appearance label, even for one continuously visible person. The tracker now tries a second, conservative match for unmatched detections when the boxes remain horizontally connected, their centers stay nearby relative to body size, and torso appearance agrees. It also accepts a large center shift during a strong, unique appearance match, and retains a lost track for up to two seconds. After a miss longer than 400 ms, appearance must agree before a label can be reused, even if a new person occupies the same box.

Face association no longer requires 70% of the central face region to exceed 0.65 mask confidence. It first uses clear high-confidence ownership, then a lower-confidence mask at 0.35, and finally a unique head/torso geometry match when the segmentation mask omits the head. Competing masks or two plausible people still make the face ambiguous. Synthetic cases cover weak face masks, missing head masks, and a torso box that starts below the face. A packaged browser smoke associated both clear bus faces and the side-facing woman; the combined heavy smoke timed out on an unrelated no-person image during cold model startup, while the smaller face/profile run passed. Real user footage and standing-to-push-up footage remain untested.

Regression tests reproduce standing-to-prone and crouching transitions, a 1.5-second detector gap, and a different-looking replacement person. These are tracker-level tests; the included browser fixtures do not contain a real push-up video, so model detection quality and mask alignment on that motion remain to be checked with footage of the reported case.

## September 30 optional body-gender models

The popup now offers separate image and video selectors for the original YuNet/FastFace path, Intel 0230 body attributes, PaddleClas PPLCNet body attributes, or either face-first fallback. The original face path remains the default. Body-only video skips high-resolution face capture and YuNet/FastFace inference unless face matching or diagnostics separately needs them. In a face-first fallback, a person without an accepted face estimate receives one body estimate; an accepted face vote is not immediately overridden while video tracker evidence accumulates. The existing confidence threshold and tracker evidence rules still apply, so an uncertain person stays censored under the default unknown-person policy.

The converted ONNX models matched native labels on the 40 back-view PA-100K probe crops: Intel 33/40 and Paddle 37/40. Warm isolated packaged-Chromium WASM inference was about 13.5 ms Intel and 25.7 ms Paddle per person crop, excluding detection, capture, preprocessing, and tracking. ## October 1 face-mask association correction

The prior face association combined face-mask coverage with a shoulder-neighborhood score before choosing a person. It could rank nearby shoulders above the actual mask owner, and fixed soft-mask/head-box acceptance paths could bypass the Face/person mask coverage slider. The revised path ranks direct face-mask ownership first, applies the slider to every mask-based match in both overlap and center modes, and uses a unique head/shoulder geometry fallback only when no face-mask pixels are present. Competing mask owners and duplicate face claims still remain unclassified. Targeted tests cover partial coverage at strong and weak mask confidence, center mode, a misleading shoulder patch, missing head masks, and overlapping owners. A packaged browser smoke still associated and classified both clear bus faces and the side-facing woman. This does not prove that every detected face will receive a label: face detection, appearance confidence, and track continuity can each independently keep a person unclassified.

## October 1 body-only mode verification

The body-only modes do not invoke FastFace. Packaged browser runs with image skin-and-hair coverage and fast-box video used Paddle-only images plus Intel-only video, then Intel-only images plus Paddle-only video. Each selected model made three body attempts and zero face-gender attempts; neither medium detected a face in those runs. With whole-body image coverage, image face detection still located two faces for mask openings while the body-only image mode made zero face-gender attempts. Switching body → face → body on active media returned both image and video to zero face-gender attempts. The debug overlay now identifies such face boxes as locations only, and an idle FastFace session is released when both media modes are body-only.

The raw model outputs are a separate accuracy limitation. On the repo's three clear bus-person crops, Intel assigned one male-looking person a female score of 0.945, while Paddle's male scores for two people were below the default 0.85 acceptance threshold. These are body-model predictions, not face-model results. The default unknown-person policy keeps low-confidence predictions censored; an accepted wrong prediction can still choose the wrong filter outcome. This fixture is too small to calibrate new thresholds or establish reliability.
